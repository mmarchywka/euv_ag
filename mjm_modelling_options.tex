\subsection{ Organic Photochemistry Benchmark Systems }
A search for modelling approach will require some known
baseline systems for validation. These could include simple absorption
spectra in a relevant spectral range, secondary electron characteristics,
or more direct measures of photoresists performance such as 
chemical reaction parameters. 
The models should address open issues relevant to resists,
and as of 2017 there were many fundamentals such as electrons/photon
not being known
\cite{AmritNarasimhan2017}. However, fundamental processes
such as Auger or autoionization need to be explored and perhaps
novel approaches to resist chemistry can be designed. The prior work
seems ignore issues beyond depth of electron and plasmon generation. 
A 2021 exploration of Sb and halogen containing resists demonstrated good contrast
curves but details remain a mystery although outgassing of organic
bromines was observed \cite{MichaelMurphy2021} suggestive of
chemistry similar to silver halides in some conditions.
A 2012 overview of EUV resists from Sematech  considered hafnium and appears to
accept as fact that the initial interaction is to generate one electron
from a core state that may thermalize \cite{NeisserMark2012}.


In one work, scission and crosslinking rates were measured for some common
polymers such as PMMA at various UV lithography wavelengths
from 248 to 13.4 nm \cite{RobertDAllen_Polymer_photochemistry_2010}.

Outgassing rates in response to 193,13.5, and 6.7 nm
exposure have also been measured in PMMA and a PAG compound
evaluated by type of outgassing molecule and average creation
energy required \cite{HO2018306}.
In the work cited earlier, m/z of PMMA fragments outgassing under 92eV
radiation were found that could be easily attributed to specific bond
scission 
\cite{DouglasGuerreroadditionalGillesRAmblard_Dissociative_photoionization_2023}.
There do not appear to be works looking at high spectral resolution
response of any photoresist that may reveal resonance or other features
that could explain any confusing behaviors and be easy to tune
for beneficial response.

Recently interest has increased around tin and other metal clusters
. A 2025 work on tin oxide clusters investigated their response 
to  EUV and e-beam exposure with techniques such as TGA, IT absorption,
X{S, and contract curves \cite{D4NA00651H}.
Metal oxide clusters vaguely resemble silver halide grains.
While high resolution EUV absorption spectrscopy is lacking apaprently,
a curve from 20 to 100eV of a tin-oxo cage resist did show decent agreement
with observed absorption at 92eV with an extrapolation of the curve
from about 70eV
\cite{Sadegh_Evrard_Kraus_Absorption_Spectroscopy_2024}.
